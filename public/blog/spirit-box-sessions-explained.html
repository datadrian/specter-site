Spirit Box Explained: How Ghost Box Sessions Actually Work

TOPICAudio equipment
READ TIME~5 min
PUBLISHEDJuly 29, 2026
TYPICAL COST$50-100 retail

A spirit box gives you something to listen to in the moment: a fast scan across radio frequencies, producing fragments of static and stray broadcast content that investigators listen to for word-like responses. What it doesn't give you is anything to look back at afterward. Here's how the device actually works, what it can't do on its own, and why the recording you make of the session matters more than what you hear live.

1.0What a Spirit Box Actually Does

A spirit box, also called a ghost box, sweeps rapidly across AM or FM radio frequencies, landing on each one only long enough to grab a fraction of a second of whatever is broadcasting there before jumping to the next. The result is a stream of static punctuated by fragments of real stations: a syllable, a snippet of a song, a burst of talk radio. Investigators ask questions and listen for fragments that seem to respond.

There's no detection circuitry involved, no sensor reading anything paranormal. It's a radio scanning across real broadcast content faster than normal listening, and the pattern-matching happens entirely in the listener's ear in the moment.

2.0What It Can't Do

A spirit box has no memory of its own. Whatever it produces plays once, out loud, in the room, and is gone the instant it happens unless something else is recording it. It can't log a timestamp, it can't tell you whether a camera caught anything at that same second, and it gives you no way to go back and listen to a fragment more closely, slower, or as a visual spectrogram.

What it can't do: remember anything, correlate with any other instrument, or survive the session as evidence unless you separately record it.

3.0The Session Isn't the Evidence, the Recording Is

Because a spirit box session is live audio with nothing stored, the actual evidence from a session is whatever recording you happened to be running alongside it. That recording is what lets you slow a fragment down, view it as a spectrogram to separate speech-like content from scanning static, and check the exact second it happened against everything else running in the room at the same time.

7.0Frequently Asked Questions

How does a spirit box work?
A spirit box, sometimes called a ghost box, rapidly scans across AM or FM radio frequencies, producing short bursts of static and fragments of whatever stations it passes over. Investigators listen for word-like fragments that seem to respond to questions, though the fragments are, by design, pulled from scanning across real broadcast content.
Is a spirit box the same as EVP?
They're related but different. EVP (electronic voice phenomenon) refers to a voice found on a quiet recording afterward. A spirit box instead plays live, audible fragments during the session itself by scanning radio frequencies, so anything heard happens in real time rather than being discovered later on playback.
What are the limitations of a spirit box?
A spirit box has no memory. Whatever it produces plays once, in the room, and is gone unless someone is separately recording it. It also can't log a timestamp, correlate a fragment with what a camera or sensor caught at that same moment, or let you review the raw audio later at a slower speed or as a spectrogram.
Should I record a spirit box session?
Yes. Recording the session is what actually makes it reviewable: it lets you go back and listen slower, look at a spectrogram of the fragment, and check whether anything else in the room, a camera, a thermal reading, or a field sensor, logged something at that exact timestamp.
Does SPECTER replace a spirit box?
No, SPECTER doesn't scan radio frequencies. It's a separate layer that records and time-syncs whatever audio happens during a session, including a spirit box running nearby, against your camera, thermal, and sensor evidence, so any fragment worth reviewing can be checked against everything else that was captured at that same moment.
