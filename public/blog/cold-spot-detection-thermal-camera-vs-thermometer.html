Cold Spots Explained: What Causes Them and How to Actually Detect One

TOPICEquipment comparison
READ TIME~5 min
PUBLISHEDJuly 29, 2026
THERMAL CAMERAWorks over RTSP

"Cold spot" is one of the oldest reports in the field, and one of the easiest to get wrong with the wrong tool. A handheld thermometer can tell you one point is cold. It can't tell you the shape of the cool area, whether it has a defined edge, or whether it's just a draft from the door you didn't notice. Here's the difference between chasing a cold spot by hand and actually imaging one.

1.0What a Cold Spot Report Actually Means

A cold spot is a localized area that reads noticeably colder than the space around it, with no obvious source like an open window, a vent, or a draft under a door. The key word is localized: a whole room being cold isn't a cold spot, it's just a cold room. What makes a reading worth investigating is a defined, bounded area of temperature drop that doesn't line up with an ordinary airflow source.

2.0Why a Pocket Thermometer Falls Short

A handheld infrared thermometer reads exactly one point, wherever you aim it, one reading at a time. That's enough to confirm a spot is cold, but not enough to see its shape, its edges, or whether it's actually a thin stream of air moving across the room from a door or vent you didn't clock. Sweeping a handheld unit point by point across a wall is slow, and by the time you've covered the area, whatever caused the reading may have already moved or dissipated.

What it can't do: show a whole scene at once, capture a shape or a moving boundary, or log the reading anywhere beyond whatever number you wrote down.

3.0What a Thermal Camera Sees Instead

A thermal imaging camera images an entire scene as a temperature map, all at once, in real time. A localized cool region shows up immediately as a visibly distinct shape against the rest of the frame, so you can see its edges, whether it's moving, and whether it traces back to a vent, window, or doorway before treating it as anything unusual. That's the difference between a single number and an actual picture of what's happening across the space.

7.0Frequently Asked Questions

What causes a cold spot in ghost hunting?
Investigators use "cold spot" to describe a localized area that reads noticeably colder than the surrounding space with no obvious source, like a draft, an open window, or an HVAC vent. A single point reading isn't enough to confirm one, since drafts and airflow are the most common ordinary explanation and can only be ruled out by seeing the shape and edges of the temperature drop.
Can an infrared thermometer detect a cold spot?
A handheld infrared thermometer only reads one small point at a time, wherever you aim it. It can confirm a single spot is cold, but it can't show you the shape or edges of the cool area, whether it's moving, or whether it lines up with a draft source, which makes it easy to miss the difference between a real localized anomaly and ordinary airflow.
How does a thermal camera find a cold spot?
A thermal camera images an entire scene as a temperature map at once, so a localized cool region shows up as a visibly distinct shape against the rest of the frame in real time. That makes it possible to see whether the cool area has a defined edge, whether it moves, and whether it lines up with a vent, window, or doorway before treating it as anything unexplained.
